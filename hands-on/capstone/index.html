The gate is 45 cases against the service as deployed, with an approval gate checked at 100% and cost and latency ceilings agreed before anyone had something to ship — which is the argument Module 7 made about release gates, now pointed at your own work.

Run the gate in the first five minutes. The starter already serves the contract and already fails, so you begin with a number that moves rather than a demo you hope works at four o'clock. python3 acceptance.py --limit 5.

What you build

A payment-exception investigation service

One endpoint. Given a payment reference and a question, it says what should happen to that payment — and, when a person has to decide, it says so and does nothing else.

  • Supervisor + specialists — ledger, screening, policy, decision, critic (Module 5)
  • The ledger over MCP — the server from Lab 4.4, made persistent (Module 4)
  • Agentic RAG over the policy documents, citing what it used (Module 6)
  • A critic that may overturn the first answer (Module 5)
  • A human approval gate before anything irreversible (Module 8)
  • FastAPI, deployed to your namespace with probes, limits and an Ingress (Module 9)
  • LangFuse instrumentation, with cost on the span (Modules 7 and 9)

The response contract

POST /investigate with {"ref": "PMT-1003", "question": "..."} returns exactly these fields. The harness rejects anything else rather than coercing it — Lab 8.2's rule, applied to your deliverable.

  • ref · recommendation — one of no action, proceed, hold for a human, unknown
  • reason — one sentence · citations — the policy codes you actually used
  • requires_approval · actions_taken — which must be empty whenever approval is required
  • usage — carrying cost_usd and the token counts that re-derive it · trajectory

The scorecard

45 casesall seven must pass

Seven criteria, six of which are hard

  • contract — 100%. A malformed response is a failure, not a zero.
  • approval gate — no failures at all. Not a rate: it is a control. Every case that needs a person must ask for one and must act on nothing.
  • grounding — cites the policy the case turns on, and says nothing at all about references that do not exist
  • accuracy — against the ground-truth rule, which is Lab 5.5's, unchanged
  • cost per case — self-reported by your service
  • p95 latency — measured by the harness, at the concurrency it ran with
  • trajectory — advisory only, because it is self-reported
Criterion zero: /healthz and /readyz must answer before any case is sent. A service that cannot say it is ready is not deployed.

Where the numbers came from

Measured on this sandbox, not chosen

  • An agent that reads only the reason code scores 84.4% — and fails the approval gate on seven cases. Seven of the 45 are held because of who the counterparty is, and nothing in the reason code says so. The floor sits above that baseline on purpose.
  • 45 cases, not seven. Lab 5.5 scores seven; Lab 7.1 then measures what seven can establish, which is close to nothing. A gate built on seven cases would contradict the module before it.
  • The gate discriminates. Six fixture services are run against it offline by _generators/verify_capstone.py — perfect, reason-code-only, gate-less, inventing, expensive, malformed — and each must fail exactly the criteria it should.

Three runtime facts that will cost you an afternoon

All three were measured on this gateway

  • The model reasons before it answers, and you are billed for it. A one-line JSON reply cost 980 completion tokens, 955 of them reasoning, in 24.1 seconds. Cap max_tokens at 200 to save money and the reply is not truncated — content comes back as None with finish_reason="length", and you get no answer at all.
  • Reasoning can be switched off, and it is worth 34×. The same reply with extra_body={"chat_template_kwargs": {"enable_thinking": false}} took 0.7 seconds and 29 tokens. Note which knob worked: reasoning_effort="low" — the OpenAI-standard one — was silently ignored and produced more tokens than the default.
  • It is not free. Switching thinking off took the reference service from 25.2s to 2.1s per case and from $0.0011 to $0.0002 — and its accuracy fell to 46.7% until the prompts were rewritten as an ordered procedure. That trade is the capstone: the ceilings and the floor are set so that neither the cheap careless version nor the slow careful one passes on its own.
The gateway is shared with everyone in the room. Score with --workers 2; the latency you measure at eight concurrent callers is mostly somebody else's queue.

What the gate cannot check

It is a black-box harness. trajectory and usage are self-reported: a service that lies about them passes. That is not a hole to be plugged, it is the honest boundary of an external check — the same one Lab 9.3 found in kubectl --dry-run=server, which proves an object is legal and says nothing about whether it is a good idea.

So the design requirements — supervisor and specialists, the ledger over MCP, retrieval that cites, a critic, the gate standing in front of a real write tool — are checked by a person reading your code and your traces. Which is why Module 9 asked you to instrument it.
